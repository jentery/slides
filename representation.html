Representation + Media

Representation in media is a form of social control.

Media demonstrate power through exclusion.

Media reflect cultural power through stereotypes.

Here, I'm drawing from the work of Stuart Hall.

Representation + Media

Media use genre to normalize stereotypes.

Audiences internalize stereotypes.

Audiences use appropriation, counter-types, and deconstruction to transcode stereotypes.

Personal Identity

How, in the context of Media Studies, we use
formats, genres, and content to define ourselves.

Examples include "cozy gamer," "sci fi nerd,"
"fantasy geek," "Swiftie," and cosplay.

Social Identity

Positionality: "the personal values, views, and
location in time and space that influence how
one engages with and understands the world.
It is wrapped up in the dynamics of power and
privilege" (Darla Benton Kearney)

When, for instance, are you in a position
where you don't notice stereotypes?

Source: UDL for IDEA

Social Identity

Intersectionality: "acknowledges the complex
relationship between social identities, and systems
of power and oppression" (Darla Benton Kearney)

How, for instance, might a stereotype articulate
race, gender, and sexuality?

Source: UDL for IDEA
Kimberlé W. Crenshaw coined the term, "intersectionality," in 1989.

"The following graphic entitled 'Intersectionality'
visually displays how social identities intersect
with one another and are wrapped in systems of
power. For example, using the imagery of a
spirograph, [Sylvia] Duckworth (2020)
colour-codes various social identities
including race, ethnicity, gender identity,
class, language, religion, ability, sexuality,
mental health, age, education, and body size."

Source: UDL for IDEA

Personal + Social

The interplay of personal and social identities
means our identifications with media — our
experiences of feeling seen — are
partial or contradictory.

I can, for instance, identify with a fantasy
character without assuming I am that character.

Personal + Social

The act of transcoding a stereotype engages
such contradictions, while vulgar expressions of
power (such as stereotypes) aim to "flatten"
people's identities for social control.
